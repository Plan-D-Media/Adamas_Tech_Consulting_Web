For two years the AI conversation belonged to large enterprises with big budgets and innovation labs. Small and mid-sized businesses asked a sharper question: will it pay for itself? That question is where the next wave of AI value will come from, and the answer is less about technology than about operations.
## The value gap, in numbers
Most AI investment has not yet reached the P&L. Across the largest studies of the past year, the pattern is the same: plenty of pilots, very little production.
## Only 1 in 12 enterprise AI evaluations reaches production
Share of organisations reaching each stage (percent)

MIT Project NANDA, The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025 · 300+ deployments, 52 interviews, 153 leaders

The pattern holds at the top. In
[link: "PwC’s 29th Global CEO Survey of 4,454 CEOs, 56% saw no significant financial benefit from AI, and only 12% saw both cost and revenue gains." -> https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-global-ceo-survey.html]
The next wave is at risk too. Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, citing rising costs, unclear business value and weak risk controls.
  (link: "Gartner" -> https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
## The lesson is not that AI fails. It is that AI which cannot be run, measured and governed in production never gets the chance to pay back.
## Why SMBs are the next wave
Smaller firms are better placed to cross the gap than their enterprise peers. MIT found mid-market organisations move from pilot to full implementation in about 90 days, against nine months or more for large enterprises (Virtualization Review).
  (link: "Virtualization Review)." -> https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)
Adoption is already broad. In Salesforce’s SMB Trends survey, 75% of SMBs were at least experimenting with AI, and 91% of those using it said it boosts revenue. What separates winners from the rest is not whether they use AI, but what they build underneath it.
  (link: "Salesforce’s SMB Trends survey" -> https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/smbs-ai-trends-2025/)

## Stuck in the pilot trap
Pilots stall for four reasons, and only three of them are about technology. A promising proof of concept, an impressive demo, genuine excitement in the room. Then months pass and the pilot is still a pilot: admired in meetings, absent from daily operations.

## Closing the gap is an operations problem
The firms that get paid back from AI have better foundations, not better models. PwC found CEOs with strong AI foundations, such as responsible AI frameworks and integrated technology, are three times more likely to report meaningful financial returns (
[link: "PwC" -> https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-global-ceo-survey.html]
). MIT traced the failures to systems that do not retain feedback, adapt to context or improve over time (
[link: "Virtualization Review" -> https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx]
).
Both findings point to the same fix: treat AI as a running service, with a data foundation beneath it and an operating loop around it.
